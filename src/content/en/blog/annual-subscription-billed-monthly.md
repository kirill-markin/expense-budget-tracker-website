A subscription can charge you every month and still commit you to a full year. That matters when you cancel: the confirmation may stop the next annual renewal while leaving several monthly payments on the way. Remove those payments from your budget too soon, and money that looks available already has a job.

For an **annual subscription billed monthly**, keep the posted charges in your expense history and the remaining installments in a separate payment schedule. Before changing that schedule, confirm what cancellation actually ends: renewal, the current commitment, or access to the service.

## A monthly price can mean different things

The billing interval tells you when the merchant collects money. The commitment tells you how long you've agreed to pay. Read both in the plan details or purchase confirmation.

| Plan | How money leaves your account | What to check before removing future payments |
|---|---|---|
| Month-to-month | One charge per billing month | Cancellation cutoff and the final bill under your plan |
| Annual, prepaid | The year's charge is collected upfront | Whether any refund applies and whether renewal is off |
| Annual, paid monthly | The annual commitment is collected in installments | Remaining payments, any early-termination option, and the next annual renewal |

A prepaid annual plan might advertise a monthly equivalent, such as €20 a month, while collecting €240 at once. An annual plan paid monthly might actually collect €20 each month. They have the same hypothetical annual price but very different payment schedules.

If you still need to find your subscriptions across cards and app stores, start with the [subscription tracking guide](/blog/how-to-track-subscriptions/). For this exercise, take one confirmed annual commitment and work out exactly what's left.

## Work through one complete year

Suppose a fictional service costs €20 per month for a 12-month commitment starting June 15, 2026. It charges on the 15th, with the first payment at signup. The total is €240, including all taxes and fees in this example, with no price changes or missed payments.

You review it on September 21, 2026. Four payments have posted. You turn off renewal that day, and the confirmation says the eight remaining installments will continue. Access lasts through June 14, 2027; the next annual term would have started June 15, 2027.

| Payment | Charge date | Amount | Status on September 21 |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | June 15, 2026 | €20 | Posted |
| 2 | July 15, 2026 | €20 | Posted |
| 3 | August 15, 2026 | €20 | Posted |
| 4 | September 15, 2026 | €20 | Posted |
| 5 | October 15, 2026 | €20 | Scheduled |
| 6 | November 15, 2026 | €20 | Scheduled |
| 7 | December 15, 2026 | €20 | Scheduled |
| 8 | January 15, 2027 | €20 | Scheduled |
| 9 | February 15, 2027 | €20 | Scheduled |
| 10 | March 15, 2027 | €20 | Scheduled |
| 11 | April 15, 2027 | €20 | Scheduled |
| 12 | May 15, 2027 | €20 | Scheduled |

The numbers reconcile: **€80 posted + €160 remaining = €240 for the term**. Of the remaining amount, €60 falls in October–December 2026 and €100 in January–May 2027.

September already contains its €20 charge. Don't add another September installment when building the future schedule. The last charge is May 15, even though service continues into June. Payment date, service end date, and renewal date each answer a different question.

Turning off renewal leaves this entire schedule intact. It prevents a new term from starting; it doesn't make the remaining €160 available for other spending.

## Check what the cancellation screen promises

These two real policies show why the confirmation matters. The examples below were checked on September 21, 2026.

Selected third-party services offer monthly subscriptions with a 12-month commitment through Apple. Apple says canceling prevents automatic renewal, while remaining payments and service access continue until the commitment ends. This billing option through Apple isn't available in the United States or Singapore. Its guidance says to cancel at least one day before renewal. See [Apple's commitment and cancellation details](https://support.apple.com/en-us/126901).

Adobe describes a different outcome for its annual plan paid monthly: cancellation after 14 days carries an early-termination fee of 50% of the remaining contract balance. That help page explicitly applies to **US subscribers**. See [Adobe's US subscription terms](https://helpx.adobe.com/account/individual/terms-policies-and-regulations/adobe-subscription-terms.html).

Read the terms for your own plan, seller, and country. Save the confirmation with its effective date, remaining charges, and service end date. A status reading “canceled” alone doesn't tell you which future amounts to remove.

### When a termination quote replaces the installments

Consider an alternative to the fictional schedule above. Suppose the merchant offers to end the commitment for a single €80 payment on September 21 and explicitly confirms that this replaces all eight remaining installments. This is an illustrative quote, not a price offered by Apple or Adobe.

Your future payment plan changes from €160 spread across eight months to €80 due now. The €80 already posted remains in your history. Once the termination charge posts, total spending on the ended subscription is €160: €80 in regular installments plus €80 to terminate.

Remove the eight future installments only after accepting the offer and receiving confirmation. Keeping both those installments and the replacement fee would overstate future payments by €160. Removing them before the agreement is confirmed could understate what you still need.

A smaller total can also mean a larger immediate cash requirement. Check the quoted charge date and the date access ends before deciding.

## Keep the commitment beside your expense history

A small note or spreadsheet is enough. For the original example, its entry after renewal cancellation would look like this:

| Field | Recorded value |
|---|---|
| Service and billing account | Fictional service; household debit account |
| Commitment | June 15, 2026–June 14, 2027 |
| Installments | 12 × €20; four posted |
| Remaining | Eight payments; €160 total |
| Next / last charge | October 15, 2026 / May 15, 2027 |
| Renewal | Off; confirmed September 21, 2026 |
| Evidence | Saved purchase terms and cancellation confirmation |

Include €20 in each affected month's spending plan. If your subscription category already includes this payment, keep it there; don't add another €20 from the commitment table. Add the dates to your [bill calendar](/blog/how-to-use-a-bill-calendar-for-budgeting/) so the paying account has enough cash when each charge arrives. A plan doesn't reserve money by itself; check that it fits alongside your other bills.

After October's charge posts, the expense history becomes €100 and the remaining commitment becomes €140. Their sum is still €240. Repeat that update using actual charges, rather than reducing the balance merely because a calendar month has passed. A missed or changed charge needs checking against the merchant's account.
