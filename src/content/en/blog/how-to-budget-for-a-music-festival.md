In the hypothetical plan below, a $400 festival pass needs a $960 trip budget once you include camping, travel, food, supplies, extras, and a buffer. Saving that amount by departure still doesn't solve the first problem: the ticket and a shared campsite require $640 at checkout, and you have $300 ready.

To build a music festival budget, price the whole trip, calculate your share of group costs, and put every payment beside a date. Then check the money available at each deadline. That gives you both a realistic trip total and a booking decision you can actually use.

## Check what the pass actually buys

Check the official pages for the days and areas your ticket covers, whether camping is included, and what your vehicle needs to enter.

For example, [Bonnaroo's 2026 accommodation information](https://www.bonnaroo.com/accommodations) describes car camping sold per car or campsite, with a separate Centeroo ticket for each person. Specialty programs differ. This illustrates a booking structure, not future prices.

Use the final checkout total for each item. Don't add a generic fee allowance on top of fees already included. [Ticketmaster's US pricing guidance](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/9663528775313-How-are-ticket-prices-and-fees-determined) says upfront prices include required fees, generally before taxes; some delivery choices can add a charge.

Build the rest of the trip around your actual arrival and departure:

| Part of the plan | What to price |
| --- | --- |
| Admission | Correct ticket type, tax, selected delivery, any separate early-entry purchase |
| Somewhere to sleep | Campsite or room, number of nights, included equipment, refundable deposits |
| Getting there and home | Fuel or fares, tolls, parking or shuttle, luggage, a safe journey home |
| Equipment | Items you still need after checking what you can borrow, including ear protection and weather protection |
| Food and water | Travel-day meals, campsite groceries, onsite meals, a water plan that follows the event's rules |
| Other needs | Showers, charging, childcare or pet care where relevant |
| Optional spending | Merchandise, drinks, upgrades, other purchases you choose |

Count early-arrival meals and any extra night before comparing camping with a hotel. Read the allowed-item, food, water, and re-entry policies before planning to bring meals or leave for supplies. Keep refundable deposits separate from expected spending but include them in the cash needed until repayment.

If the trip means unpaid time off, reduce the income available in the affected month. Don't also enter that lost pay as a purchase; that would count the same effect twice.

## A music festival budget for one person

Here is an illustrative plan for three festival days plus one travel day, with you and two friends sharing one campsite. The worksheet's dollar amounts and payment dates are hypothetical. They are not average festival prices or a Bonnaroo quote.

Assume the group already owns the main camping equipment, and the four-day food allowance covers the full trip, including the return journey. Add another day's costs if your arrival or journey home extends the trip.

| Item | Calculation | Your planned amount |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Admission | Final checkout total for one person | $400 |
| Campsite | $240 shared equally by three people | $80 |
| Travel | Your share of the complete return journey | $120 |
| Equipment and supplies | Remaining items after borrowing | $60 |
| Food and water | Four days at $40 | $160 |
| Optional extras | One limit for the trip | $40 |
| Contingency | Money held for unplanned needs | $100 |
| **Total to fund** | | **$960** |

The planned purchases total $860. The extra $100 remains available if something costs more than expected; it isn't a purchase you must make.

Replace the figures with checkout totals and grounded estimates. Mark unknown food prices as provisional. Check that contributions fit alongside your regular commitments using your [available fun-money budget](/blog/how-to-budget-fun-money/).

## The total fits, but checkout doesn't

Suppose you already have $300 reserved and can put aside $110 on each of six paydays before departure:

`$300 + (6 × $110) = $960`

The total works. But imagine checkout is February 1, your first payday is February 15, and you're buying your $400 admission plus the entire $240 campsite. The merchant needs $640 immediately.

Your personal campsite cost is $80, but you must front the other $160 until friends pay. Checkout leaves a **$340 cash gap**. Collecting $80 from each friend beforehand reduces that gap to $180.

One workable version would require finding that additional $180 in money you can genuinely reassign before February 1. You would then have $480 of your own money plus $160 from friends, enough for checkout. Because you've moved $180 of your saving forward, your remaining six contributions fall from $110 to $80.

This is how that revised plan could run:

| Illustrative date | Money available or payment | Festival money left |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Before February 1 | $300 already reserved + $180 reassigned + $160 received from friends | $640 |
| February 1 | Pay $400 admission and $240 campsite | $0 |
| February 15 and March 1 | Reserve $80 on each payday | $160 |
| March 2 | Buy $60 of equipment and supplies | $100 |
| March 15 and April 1 | Reserve another $80 on each payday | $260 |
| April 15 and May 1 | Reserve the final two $80 contributions | $420 |
| May 8–11 | Allow $120 for travel, $160 for food, and $40 for extras | $100 contingency remaining |

Your own funding is still `$300 + $180 + (6 × $80) = $960`. The friends' $160 goes toward their share of camping. It doesn't increase your spending allowance.

Travel is paid during the trip here. Move any prepaid fares to their actual dates and recalculate. If the extra $180 isn't available, choose a cheaper complete plan or a booking/payment schedule you can fund. Money arriving in April cannot pay a February charge.

For the money you set aside between bookings, the [sinking-fund guide](/blog/how-to-track-sinking-funds/) explains how to keep the reserve visible without treating every contribution as spending.

## Read a festival payment plan as a list of bills

Copy the actual installment schedule before confirming. [Bonnaroo's layaway guidance](https://support.bonnaroo.com/hc/en-us/articles/4402712768404-Layaway-plans-overview) says amounts and dates depend on when you buy and appear at final checkout; follow-up payments are automatically drawn from the same payment method.

Record every installment, any plan charge, payment method, and cancellation or missed-payment terms. Compare the total with paying in full. Add calendar dates and check the account balance after other bills.

For each deadline, use:

`available festival money = starting reserve + contributions received + friends' payments received - payments already made`

The balance after the next charge must stay at or above zero. Promised transfers don't count. Neither does hoped-for resale income: [Ticketmaster says some events and tickets aren't eligible for resale](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/9672915828881-How-do-I-sell-tickets), and an eligible listing still needs a buyer.

## Agree on what happens if someone drops out

The $80 campsite share depends on three people paying. If one person leaves the group before contributing and the remaining two cover the unchanged $240 booking, each now owes $120. Your plan rises by $40, to $1,000 if you preserve the full $100 contingency.

If you keep the original $960 funding target instead, that extra $40 leaves only $60 of contingency. Write down that choice rather than leaving the original buffer in the table as though nothing changed. Shared fuel or a room may need recalculating too.

Before one person books, agree in writing on:

- Who pays the merchant and when everyone sends their share.
- Whether a departing friend remains responsible for an already committed cost.
- What happens if a replacement joins, the booking changes, or a refund arrives.

Check the seller's transfer and cancellation rules too. Record each person's share, payment received, and balance owed; see [tracking reimbursable expenses](/blog/how-to-track-reimbursable-expenses/) for keeping personal costs separate from money you front.

## Turn the remaining money into a daily allowance

In the example, travel has its own $120 allocation. Food and optional extras have $200 between them, while the $100 contingency stays separate.

You could use the $200 like this:

- Travel day: $40 for food.
- Three festival days: $40 per day for food, totaling $120.
- Optional extras: $40 across all three festival days, about $13.33 per day on average.

That gives roughly $53.33 per festival day for food and optional purchases. Treat it as a limit to test against your event's food prices and your needs, not a recommended daily amount for every festival.

If you want a $30 shirt, it uses $30 of the $40 extras budget. You have $10 left for other extras; the food money still has a job. Buying campsite groceries in advance uses part of the food allowance too, so subtract those purchases before setting your onsite limit.

Trim upgrades, merchandise, or trip length when needed. Keep food, water, ear protection, and safe transport funded.

## Keep the reserve and the purchases separate

Expense Budget Tracker supports monthly category plans, actual spending, accounts, and transfers. Start with the [getting-started guide](/docs/getting-started/).

Put expected purchases in the months you will pay them. Keep the festival reserve, group shares, and payment deadlines in a separate worksheet or note you update as money moves.

Moving $80 from checking to savings is a transfer. Record the merchant payment when it happens; don't count both as expenses.

Keep the full $240 campsite payment visible in your cash record, alongside the friends' $160 contribution and your $80 personal share in the group record. That way you can explain both the bank balance and your own trip cost without pretending you spent only $80 at checkout.

Before you buy, write down your full funding target, the next payment, the money available by that deadline, and your daily food and extras allowance. Book when you can cover that first charge and every later payment without taking money already needed for regular bills. In this example, that means collecting the friends' $160 and finding the extra $180 before February 1—not just reaching $960 by May.
