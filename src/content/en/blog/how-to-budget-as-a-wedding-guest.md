Three wedding invitations can fit on the fridge while competing for the same travel money. And the November wedding may need paying for before the October one. Looking only at the wedding dates misses the first decision you actually have to make: whether you can afford to book.

Build a wedding guest budget by pricing each invitation, deciding how much money you can set aside, and checking every payment date before you RSVP. Include gifts, travel, clothing, care arrangements, and any income you'll lose. All amounts and dates in the worked example below are hypothetical, not typical prices or gift recommendations.

![A friend lends a navy blazer in an apartment entryway, with a small wrapped gift on the bench](/blog/wedding-guest-budget.png)

## Price the version you would actually attend

Start with the ceremony and reception. Add a separate choice for each shower, bachelor or bachelorette trip, welcome dinner, or extra night away. Accepting one invitation doesn't put every related event into your spending plan.

[The Knot's wedding guest cost guide](https://www.theknot.com/content/wedding-guest-cost) identifies travel, lodging, attire, and gifts as major guest costs. Your own quotes matter more than an average. For each invitation, check:

- Return travel, baggage, airport transfers, fuel, parking, and transport home after the reception.
- Accommodation for the nights you need, including taxes and fees shown at checkout.
- A gift amount you choose, plus delivery or wrapping if applicable.
- Clothing costs after checking what you can reuse or borrow, including alterations or cleaning.
- Meals outside the hosted events, childcare, pet care, and other costs of being away.

Record time off beside the money. Paid leave uses available days; unpaid leave reduces expected income. If a trip means $150 less take-home pay, reduce the income in the relevant month and recalculate what you can set aside. Don't also enter that $150 as an expense: that would count its effect twice.

For a shared trip, agree whether each estimate covers you alone, a couple, or a household. A hotel room quoted for two people is easy to mistake for a per-person cost when comparing invitations.

## Put all the invitations on one sheet

Suppose you're considering these three weddings. Each column below shows your personal share, including a hotel room split for the regional wedding. The flight wedding assumes solo accommodation. No unpaid leave or optional events are included.

| Planned cost | Local, October 24 | Regional, November 21 | Flight, December 12 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Travel and local transport | $40 | $120 | $380 |
| Accommodation | $0 | $180 | $240 |
| Chosen gift allowance | $60 | $70 | $80 |
| Clothing and preparation | $0 | $30 | $40 |
| Meals outside hosted events | $20 | $60 | $90 |
| Childcare or pet care | $40 | $60 | $60 |
| **Personal cost** | **$160** | **$520** | **$890** |

You have $400 already reserved and can add $200 on October 1, November 1, and December 1. Your budget for wedding season is therefore $1,000, but only $400 is available today. The future contributions come from money left after bills and other commitments; the [fun-money budgeting guide](/blog/how-to-budget-fun-money/) can help establish that amount without allocating the same money twice.

All three invitations cost $1,570, leaving a $570 gap before any overruns. The local and regional weddings total $680, leaving a projected $320 by December 1 if you make every planned contribution. The flight wedding alone fits the season's total with $110 to spare, but its booking dates still need checking. Local plus flight costs $1,050 and doesn't fit the current plan.

Decide which celebrations matter most to you, then compare versions you could afford. A shorter stay or a shared ride may change the options. Reprice the whole wedding travel budget: a cheaper hotel with expensive transport might save nothing.

## Check the first booking, then every later payment

Now suppose you choose the local and regional weddings. The regional trip requires a $120 train ticket and a prepaid $360 shared hotel booking on September 28. Your hotel share is $180, but if you make the booking, the full $360 leaves your account.

Your share of this booking is $300. Your immediate payment is $480. The regional wedding's other $220 of costs still has to be funded later.

With only $400 reserved, you're $80 short unless your friend sends their $180 before you book. An agreement to repay you in November doesn't solve September's gap. The [shared travel expenses guide](/blog/how-to-track-shared-travel-expenses/) explains how to keep the group payment and your own share distinct.

If your friend pays first, the schedule for your own money becomes:

| Date | Change in your wedding money | Projected reserve balance |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| September 24 | Starting reserve: +$400 | $400 |
| September 28 | Your train and hotel share: −$300 | $100 |
| October 1 | Contribution: +$200 | $300 |
| October 24 | Local wedding costs: −$160 | $140 |
| November 1 | Contribution: +$200 | $340 |
| November 21 | Remaining regional costs: −$220 | $120 |
| December 1 | Contribution: +$200 | $320 |

This assumes the local costs are paid on October 24 and the remaining regional costs on November 21. Move gifts, clothing, or care deposits to their actual payment dates. The friend's $180 receipt and the full hotel charge still belong in your account record; the table shows only your own money. Their payment covers their hotel share and doesn't increase your personal spending allowance.

The final check is `$400 + (3 × $200) − $160 − $520 = $320`. Every intermediate balance must also stay at or above zero. A positive December balance can't fund a September shortfall. If these two weddings end your season and all costs settle as planned, you'll have $120 left after November 21 and can direct December's planned $200 elsewhere.

Maintain the reserve as a [sinking-fund record](/blog/how-to-track-sinking-funds/), backed by money you still have. If you want a printable starting point, the [CFPB's Your Money, Your Goals toolkit](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/your-money-your-goals/toolkit/) provides separate savings-plan, spending-tracker, and cash-flow worksheets.

## Write down what each booking commits you to

Before paying, add four fields beside each quote: amount due now, later balance and date, cancellation deadline, and amount you could lose. Save the terms you actually book, including whether a cancellation returns cash or only travel credit. Allow for refund processing time before assigning that money elsewhere.

For shared accommodation, agree who covers a cancellation or a missing guest's share. A refundable room still ties up cash until the refund arrives. A card limit or pending reimbursement isn't money in your reserve.

Keep optional events as separate rows. An extra dinner costing $90 in total for your meal and transport would reduce the projected December remainder from $320 to $230, assuming you keep all three contributions. It still has to fit on its payment date. If the event needs another hotel night or unpaid leave, include those effects before accepting it.

## Give a clear answer, then track the spending

You can keep the reply short:

> “I'd love to celebrate with you. I can make the wedding, but I won't be able to join the weekend trip.”

Or, when attendance doesn't fit:

> “Thank you for inviting me. I'm sorry I can't make the trip, and I hope you have a wonderful day.”

You don't need to send the couple your worksheet. Reply by their deadline, and tell anyone sharing bookings before they commit money on your behalf.

Setting aside a monthly contribution isn't itself a wedding expense. If you move that money between your own accounts, record a transfer; count the expense when you pay for the booking, gift, or other purchase. Keep your friend's share separate when comparing your personal costs with the plan.

After each booking, replace the estimate with the paid amount and update the remaining commitments. Before saying yes to the next invitation, check both the money left for the season and the next date cash has to leave your account.
